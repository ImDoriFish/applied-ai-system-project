# Hamster Care
This file covers routine daily, weekly and monthly care for pet hamsters, which sleep in the day and are active in the evening and at night. Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Hamster feeding
Feed commercial hamster pellets every day as the main food, rather than muesli-style mixes, which let hamsters pick out sugary bits. Add a small daily portion of washed fresh vegetables, fruit or herbs (a teaspoon or two a day). Timothy hay adds fibre and helps keep teeth healthy. Nuts, boiled egg or mealworms are occasional treats only. Hamsters are active in the evening and at night, so feed them then. Scattering or burying pellets in the bedding gives them something to do. For example: 19:00 pellets scattered in bedding plus a teaspoon of vegetables.
Source: [PDSA, "Are hamsters good pets?" (hamster care and diet)](https://pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/looking-after-your-pet/small-pets/your-hamsters-diet)

## Hamster water and habitat cleaning
Hamsters need constant access to clean, fresh water, ideally in a bottle with a metal spout. Spot clean every day to every few days, removing wet or soiled bedding and uneaten food, and clean the water bottle and food bowl. Do a full clean about once a week for small cages; large setups may only need a deep clean every few weeks (once or twice a month). When you deep clean, put some old, dry, unsoiled bedding back so the cage still smells like home. Clean when the hamster is awake, in the evening, not in the middle of the day.
Source: [PDSA, "Are hamsters good pets?" (hamster care and diet)](https://pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/looking-after-your-pet/small-pets/your-hamsters-diet)
Source: [RSPCA, "Creating the right home for your hamster"](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/rodents/hamsters/environment)

## Hamster exercise and enrichment
Most hamsters live alone; keeping them together often causes fights. Provide a large cage (at least 100 x 50 cm floor space) with at least 25 cm of bedding for burrowing. Give a solid-surface exercise wheel of at least 20 cm across for dwarf hamsters or 30 cm for Syrian hamsters. Add tubes, cardboard boxes, nest boxes and safe chew items. Keep the room quiet, at a steady 18-21 C, with lights going off at about the same time each night. Only handle a hamster when it is awake.
Source: [PDSA, "Are hamsters good pets?" (hamster care and diet)](https://pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/looking-after-your-pet/small-pets/your-hamsters-diet)
Source: [RSPCA, "Creating the right home for your hamster"](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/rodents/hamsters/environment)

## Hamster grooming
Most hamsters groom themselves and only need occasional access to a sand bath. Long-haired hamsters may need brushing once a week to remove mats and bedding. Check nails and trim only the white tip if they get too long; ask a vet if you are unsure. Give gnawing items so their always-growing teeth stay a healthy length.
Source: [RSPCA, "Hamster health and welfare"](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/rodents/hamsters/health)
Source: [PDSA, "Are hamsters good pets?" (hamster care and diet)](https://pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/looking-after-your-pet/small-pets/your-hamsters-diet)

## Hamster health care and vet visits
Hamsters hide pain and can lose condition quickly, so watch their behaviour, eating and droppings closely. Check their front teeth regularly, because overgrown teeth can stop them eating. Have any new lumps checked by a vet, as tumours are not uncommon. Common problems include wet tail (diarrhoea), abscesses, overgrown nails and teeth, and fur loss. Only give medicines a vet has prescribed for hamsters. If you go away, have a responsible person care for the hamster in its own cage.
