# Reptile Care
This file covers daily, weekly, monthly and yearly care for pet leopard geckos and bearded dragons. Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Reptile feeding
Leopard geckos eat live insects. Feed juveniles every 1-2 days and adults 2-3 times a week. Dust insects lightly with phosphorus-free calcium powder, gut-load them for at least 24 hours first, and keep a small dish of calcium in the tank at all times. Bearded dragons eat insects and leafy greens. VCA says young dragons eat once or twice daily and adults every 24-72 hours; the RSPCA says feed babies twice daily and juveniles and adults once daily, in the morning. Remove uneaten live insects after about 30 minutes. For example: 08:00 bearded dragon greens and dusted insects; 08:30 remove leftover insects.

## Reptile water and habitat cleaning
Change drinking water daily, and clean and rinse the water bowl daily. Spot-clean droppings and uneaten food as soon as you see them, at least once a day. Replace paper floor covering at least weekly and fully clean the tank once a month with a reptile-safe disinfectant. Run lights on a timer, about 12 hours on and 12 hours off, and check the hot-end and cool-end temperatures daily. Replace UVB bulbs about every 6 months, or as the maker directs, even if they still light up, because UV output drops with age.

## Reptile exercise and enrichment
Give a large tank with a warm end and a cool end, a hide at each end, and secure rocks and branches to climb. Bearded dragons also benefit from a digging box. Moving perches now and then, and scattering insects at irregular times, encourages natural activity. Handle calmly with both hands, supporting all four legs, for about 10-15 minutes at a time, and never lift a lizard by the tail (geckos can drop their tails). On warm, sunny days a bearded dragon can spend supervised time outdoors in a secure pen with shade.

## Reptile grooming
Reptiles shed their skin; young bearded dragons shed more often. Leopard geckos need a hide with damp moss to help shedding. When the skin looks dull, raise humidity slightly, for example by misting once a day. Never pull off stuck skin; soak the area in shallow, lukewarm water for about 5 minutes instead. Stuck skin on toes can cut off blood flow, so ask a vet if it stays. Wash your hands with soap and running water every time you handle, feed or clean up after a reptile, because reptiles often carry Salmonella. Keep reptiles and their supplies out of the kitchen, and do not let children under 5 handle them.

## Reptile health care and vet visits
Take your reptile to a reptile-experienced vet for a checkup at least once a year; some vets advise twice a year. Checkups usually include weighing and a fecal test for parasites. Reptiles hide illness well, so check your pet daily and weigh it regularly. Call a vet if it stops eating or becomes unusually inactive (do not assume it is just a winter slow-down), has abnormal droppings with weight loss, or shows muscle twitching or swollen legs, which can be signs of metabolic bone disease.
