# Cat Care
General daily, weekly, and yearly care guidance for kittens and adult cats. Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Cat feeding
Kittens: feed 3 to 4 small meals a day; do not leave unlimited food out, because free-feeding kittens can lead to obesity. Adult cats: at least 2 meals a day at regular times is best; some cats do better with 3 to 4 smaller meals. Treats should be no more than 5 to 10% of the diet. If a cat leaves a meal uneaten, that can be a sign of illness. For example, an adult cat could eat at 07:00 and 19:00, and a kitten at 07:00, 12:00, 17:00, and 22:00.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Feeding Times and Frequency for Your Cat"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/feeding-times-and-frequency-for-cats)
Source: [ASPCA, "General Cat Care"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/general-cat-care)

## Cat water and habitat cleaning
Fresh, clean water should be available at all times; wash and refill the water bowl daily. Scoop the litter box at least once a day. Once a week, dump all the litter, wash the box with warm water and unscented soap (no ammonia or scents), and refill with 1 to 2 inches of fresh litter. Have one litter box per cat plus one extra, and one on each floor of the home. Keep the box away from food bowls. Wash the cat's bedding often.
Source: [ASPCA, "General Cat Care"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/general-cat-care)
Source: [ASPCA, "Litter Box Problems"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/common-cat-behavior-issues/litter-box-problems)

## Cat exercise and enrichment
Play with your cat for about two or three 10 to 15 minute sessions a day. Several short sessions work better than one long one. Kittens need several short sessions a day; adults may do well with 1 to 2; seniors need slower, shorter play. Cats are most active at dawn and dusk, so morning and evening are good times. For example: 07:30, 18:00, and 21:30 (a bedtime session can help the cat sleep at night). Provide a sturdy scratching post at least 3 feet tall.
Source: [American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA), "How Often Should You Play with Your Cat?"](https://www.aaha.org/resources/how-often-should-you-play-with-your-cat/)
Source: [ASPCA, "General Cat Care"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/general-cat-care)

## Cat grooming
Brushing: 1 to 2 times a week for most cats; long-haired cats need brushing every few days. Nails: trim about every 10 days to 2 weeks (ASPCA's general page allows up to every 2 to 3 weeks). Ears: check once a week for wax, dirt, or smell. Paws: wipe gently with a damp cloth once a day and check for cuts. Teeth: brush regularly with cat toothpaste (never human toothpaste) and check gums for redness. Most cats rarely need a bath.
Source: [ASPCA, "Cat Grooming Tips"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/cat-grooming-tips)
Source: [ASPCA, "General Cat Care"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/general-cat-care)

## Cat health care and vet visits
Kittens: visit the vet during the first week at home; vaccines start no earlier than 6 weeks old and repeat every 3 to 4 weeks until at least 16 weeks old. Adult cats (about 1 to 10 years), including indoor cats: a vet exam at least once a year. Senior cats (over about 10 years): at least every 6 months. Spay or neuter by about 5 months of age. See the vet if vomiting, diarrhea, not eating, or tiredness lasts more than 2 days. Never give medicine your vet did not prescribe.
Source: [ASPCA, "General Cat Care"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/cat-care/general-cat-care)
Source: [American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA), "Frequency of veterinary visits"](https://www.aaha.org/frequency-of-veterinary-visits/)
Source: [PetMD, "How Often Do You Take Your Cat to the Veterinarian?"](https://www.petmd.com/cat/general-health/how-often-to-take-cat-to-veterinarian)
