# Pet Emergencies
These signs mean you should contact a veterinarian or emergency animal hospital right away; this app cannot help with emergencies.

## Poisoning and toxic foods
Foods that can poison pets include chocolate, coffee and caffeine, xylitol (in gum, candy, baked goods and toothpaste), grapes and raisins, onions, garlic and chives, macadamia nuts, alcohol and raw yeast dough. For cats, true lilies and daylilies are deadly. Even a small bite, pollen licked off the fur, or water from a lily vase can cause kidney failure. If you think your pet ate something toxic, note how much and contact a vet immediately. You can also call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435, open 24/7; a fee may apply.
Source: [ASPCA, "People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets)
Source: [ASPCA, "Getting to the Bottom of the Dangers of Easter Lilies"](https://www.aspca.org/news/getting-bottom-dangers-easter-lilies)
Source: [ASPCA, "ASPCA Poison Control"](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control)

## Breathing problems
Difficulty breathing is an emergency for any pet. A guinea pig breathing with its mouth open needs a vet immediately. Labored breathing can also come with bloat in dogs or heatstroke. Contact a vet immediately.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Emergency"](https://vcahospitals.com/family/specialty/departments/emergency)
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Health Problems in Guinea Pigs"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/guinea-pigs-problems)

## Bleeding, injuries and collapse
Contact a vet immediately for bleeding, vomiting blood, blood in poop or urine, a serious wound, a suspected broken leg, any eye injury, loss of consciousness, sudden weakness or not being able to move, or signs of severe pain. Hurt pets may bite. Keep your face away from their mouth and do not hug them. First aid does not replace vet care.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Emergency"](https://vcahospitals.com/family/specialty/departments/emergency)
Source: [AVMA, "First aid tips for pet owners"](https://atwork.avma.org/resources/pet-owners/emergencycare/first-aid-tips-pet-owners)

## Bloat in dogs
Bloat with twisting (GDV) is life-threatening and needs a vet within minutes to a few hours. Signs include restlessness or distress, heavy drooling, trying to vomit with nothing coming up, a swollen belly, trouble breathing, and collapse. Large, deep-chested dogs are at highest risk, but any dog can be affected. Contact a vet immediately.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Bloat: Gastric Dilatation and Volvulus in Dogs"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/gastric-dilatation-and-volvulus-in-dogs)

## Urinary blockage in cats
A cat that strains to pee, keeps trying to go, passes little or no urine, or cries out in pain may have a blocked urethra. Male cats are at highest risk. A blocked cat can die in less than 24-48 hours. Contact a vet immediately.
Source: [Cornell Feline Health Center, "Feline Lower Urinary Tract Disease"](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-lower-urinary-tract-disease)
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Emergency"](https://vcahospitals.com/family/specialty/departments/emergency)

## Rabbits and guinea pigs not eating or not pooping
A rabbit that stops eating or passes little or no poop may have GI stasis. It can become life-threatening within hours. Bloating is another warning sign. A guinea pig that stops eating, seems depressed, has diarrhea or feels cold needs a vet immediately. Contact a vet immediately.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Gastrointestinal Stasis in Rabbits"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/gastrointestinal-stasis-in-rabbits)
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Health Problems in Guinea Pigs"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/guinea-pigs-problems)

## Heatstroke
Heatstroke is an emergency. Signs include fast breathing, dry or sticky gums, unusual gum color, sluggishness, confusion and seizures. The most common cause is leaving a dog in a car. Flat-faced breeds are at extra risk. While you contact a vet immediately, pour cool (not cold) water over the head, belly, armpits and feet. Do not cover the pet with wet towels.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Heat Stroke in Dogs"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/heat-stroke-in-dogs)

## Seizures
Seizures are an emergency sign. A seizure lasting over 5 minutes, or several seizures close together, is an emergency. Also call for a first seizure, or if the pet does not recover quickly. Signs include collapsing, jerking, paddling legs, drooling and staring. Do not hold the pet; move objects away and time the seizure. Contact a vet immediately.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Emergency"](https://vcahospitals.com/family/specialty/departments/emergency)
Source: [Vets Now, "Seizures in dogs"](https://www.vets-now.com/pet-care-advice/seizures-in-dogs)

## Reptile and turtle emergencies
Contact a reptile vet immediately if a turtle has tissue sticking out of its vent or an injured shell. The same applies to a female with eggs who stops eating and becomes weak or unresponsive. Shell injuries can become infected within hours.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Common Problems in Aquatic Turtles"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/turtles-aquatic-problems)
