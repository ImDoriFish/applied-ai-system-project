# Guinea Pig Care
This file covers routine daily, weekly, monthly and yearly care for pet guinea pigs (feeding, cleaning, exercise, grooming and vet visits). Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Guinea pig feeding
Guinea pigs need unlimited grass hay at all times; each guinea pig should eat a pile of hay about twice its body size every day. They cannot make their own vitamin C, so they need vitamin C every day from vitamin C-rich vegetables (such as capsicum and leafy greens) or a daily supplement. Give fresh vegetables daily, more vegetables than fruit, after they have eaten their main food. Feed a measured amount of guinea pig pellets with stabilised vitamin C once a day, following the label (for example, one brand suggests 1/8 cup per guinea pig). Remove uneaten fresh food at the end of each day. For example: 08:00 pellets and hay top-up, 18:00 vegetables, 21:00 remove leftovers.
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "What should I feed my guinea pigs?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/other-pets/guinea-pigs/what-should-i-feed-my-guinea-pigs)

## Guinea pig water and habitat cleaning
Provide clean, fresh drinking water at all times and change it often; a water bottle helps keep droppings out. Clean the enclosure at least two to three times a week, because guinea pigs make a lot of waste. Change all the bedding completely at least once a week, since ammonia from urine can cause breathing and skin problems. Disinfect the cage, water bottles and food dishes with a guinea pig-safe disinfectant or hot water. Use solid floors with dust-free bedding, not wire floors or wood shavings.
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "Where should I keep my guinea pigs?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/other-pets/guinea-pigs/where-should-i-keep-my-guinea-pigs)
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "How should I care for my guinea pigs?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/other-pets/guinea-pigs/how-should-i-care-for-my-guinea-pigs)

## Guinea pig exercise and enrichment
Guinea pigs are herd animals and should live with at least one other guinea pig. Do not house them with rabbits. A pair needs an enclosure of at least 2 m x 0.5 m and 30 cm high, with hiding places. Most cages are too small for their exercise needs, so give time in a safe, secure play area. Ideally allow access to direct sunlight three to four times a week, always with shade and shelter. Keep a written enrichment plan (toys, foraging, social time) and rotate ideas each day.
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "Where should I keep my guinea pigs?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/other-pets/guinea-pigs/where-should-i-keep-my-guinea-pigs)

## Guinea pig grooming
Long-haired guinea pigs need brushing and any mats combed out every day. Short-haired guinea pigs need brushing about once a week, or at least every other day when shedding heavily. Clip nails at least once a month; overgrown nails can lead to painful foot sores (bumblefoot). Ask a vet to show you how to clip nails safely. Baths are rarely needed and are stressful, so only bathe when the coat is soiled or for lice treatment.
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "How should I groom my guinea pigs?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/other-pets/guinea-pigs/how-should-i-groom-my-guinea-pigs)

## Guinea pig health care and vet visits
Take new guinea pigs to a vet experienced with guinea pigs soon after you get them, then once a year for a check-up. Guinea pigs hide illness, so check their health during each grooming session. Lice and mites are common, so ask a vet about treatment if they scratch more than usual. Talk to your vet about desexing and whether worming is needed. Do not spray insecticides near their enclosure.
