# Turtle Care
This file covers daily, weekly, monthly and yearly care for pet aquatic turtles such as red-eared sliders. Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Turtle feeding
Feed healthy juvenile turtles every day and adults every 2-3 days. Offer only as much as the turtle eats in about 30-40 minutes. Aquatic turtles eat underwater. Feeding them in a separate small tank of warm water keeps the main tank cleaner. Scoop out any leftover food daily. Meat and other animal protein should be no more than two-thirds of a juvenile's diet and about half of an adult's diet. The rest should be leafy greens. Some vets suggest a multivitamin once a week and a calcium source twice a week. Never feed processed foods such as hot dogs, lunch meat or bread. For example: 09:00 feed juvenile in feeding tank; 09:40 return turtle and remove leftovers.

## Turtle water and habitat cleaning
Change tank water at least once a week, or sooner if dirty. Sources differ on how much: VCA notes many owners change 50% weekly and deep-clean every 3-4 weeks, while RSPCA Australia suggests changing 4-10% weekly to protect helpful bacteria. Warm new water before returning the turtle. Use a good filter and rinse filter parts only in tank water. Test water every 1-2 weeks. Clean the land area at least weekly. Keep water at 75-82°F (24-29°C) and check basking temperature daily. Run lights about 12 hours a day. Replace UVB bulbs every 6 months or as the maker directs.

## Turtle exercise and enrichment
Turtles need water deep enough to swim fully underwater, at least 1.5-2 times their shell length, plus a dry spot where they can climb fully out to bask. Safe live plants give hiding places and interest. Plastic plants can be dangerous if eaten. Changing food types regularly encourages eating, and an occasional feeder fish gives chasing exercise. Supervised time in direct outdoor sunlight is good when it is warm, as long as there is shade. To pick up a turtle, hold the rim of the upper shell at the front and back or at the sides. Put it back if it tries to bite or scratch.

## Turtle grooming
Turtle skin normally sheds in pieces that look whitish and fuzzy in the water. Shell plates (scutes) also normally flake off as the turtle grows. Raw or bleeding skin is not normal and needs a vet. Green algae on the shell can be brushed off from time to time, but it often means the tank needs more cleaning or better filtering. Turtles often carry Salmonella. Wash your hands with soap and running water after every handling, feeding or tank cleaning. Clean supplies away from the kitchen and pour tank water down the toilet, not the sink.
Source: [VCA Animal Hospitals, "Common Problems in Aquatic Turtles"](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/turtles-aquatic-problems)
Source: [CDC, "Reptiles and Amphibians"](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/reptiles-and-amphibians.html)

## Turtle health care and vet visits
Book a checkup with a reptile vet every year. Most vets advise not letting pet turtles hibernate; keeping them warm with 12-14 hours of light a day usually prevents it. See a vet immediately for any tissue sticking out of the vent, any shell injury, or a female with eggs who stops eating and becomes weak. Also see a vet for a soft, bumpy or uneven shell, or blood in droppings or straining.
