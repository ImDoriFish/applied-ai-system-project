# Rabbit Care
This file covers routine daily, weekly and yearly care for pet rabbits (feeding, cleaning, exercise, grooming and vet visits). Not a substitute for advice from a veterinarian.

## Rabbit feeding
Rabbits need unlimited good-quality hay or grass at all times; give at least one bundle of hay as big as the rabbit every day. Hay and grass should be about 80-85% of the diet, leafy greens about 10-15%, and pellets no more than 5%. Give a large handful of washed leafy greens daily (about 1 cup per kg of body weight) and a measured ration of pellets once a day (about 25 g per kg of body weight); do not keep topping up pellets. Fruit and carrots are occasional treats only. Rabbits are most active at dawn and dusk, so offer greens and pellets then. For example: 07:00 greens and pellets, 18:00 greens and a hay top-up.
Source: [RSPCA, "What do rabbits eat?"](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/rabbits/diet)
Source: [RSPCA Australia Knowledgebase, "What should I feed my rabbits?"](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/rabbits/what-should-i-feed-my-rabbits)

## Rabbit water and habitat cleaning
Rabbits must always have fresh, clean water. Change the water twice a day and clean the bowl or bottle every day. If you use a bottle, check daily that the spout is not blocked. Remove soiled bedding every day and disinfect the hutch once a week. Give one litter tray per rabbit plus one extra, filled with hay. Check each rabbit's bottom at least once a day (twice a day for at-risk rabbits in warm weather) to help prevent flystrike.

## Rabbit exercise and enrichment
Rabbits need daily supervised exercise outside their hutch, plus an attached run big enough to run, jump and stand up. A pair of rabbits needs at least 3 m x 2 m x 1 m of space. Rabbits are social and should live with at least one compatible rabbit. Give daily foraging enrichment, such as scattering food or hiding it in cardboard boxes or paper. Provide a digging box, tunnels and a raised lookout platform. Indoor rabbits benefit from safe outdoor time three to four times a week. Introduce new toys slowly.

## Rabbit grooming
Short-haired rabbits usually need brushing once a week, and more often while moulting. Long-haired rabbits must be combed every day (about 20 to 40 minutes), or clipped every four to six weeks and still combed daily. Grooming removes loose hair that could cause hairballs and is a good time for a quick health check. Trim nails when they grow too long; a vet can show you how. Keep grooming sessions short and reward calm behaviour.

## Rabbit health care and vet visits
Take rabbits for a vet check-up once a year, including a dental check, and register with a rabbit-savvy vet. Vaccination against rabbit haemorrhagic disease (calicivirus) is given every 6 to 12 months; ask your vet which vaccines apply where you live. Desexing is recommended. Use only vet-approved flea products, because fipronil (Frontline) can kill rabbits. Watch appetite and droppings every day. If droppings change and do not return to normal within 24 hours, book a vet visit.
